When companies ask for an audit

Most wasted security spending starts before anything is installed.

These are the situations where a written audit is worth more than a quotation. If yours is here, the audit gives you a plan you can budget against.

After a break-in or theft

Something was taken, and nobody can say for certain how they got in.

We trace the likely route, check what failed along it, and list what would have stopped it.

In the report: entry routes and failed controls

Guards on site, but losses continue

You pay for guards every month, and stock or materials still leave the site.

We check patrol coverage, gate records, key control, and shift handovers, not just whether a guard is present.

In the report: guarding and procedure findings

Three quotations that do not match

Each installer recommends different equipment, and you cannot tell who is right.

The audit gives you one list of what the site needs, which you can take to any installer or guard company.

In the report: a ranked list of fixes

A budget that needs evidence

Management or the board wants to know why the money is needed before approving it.

Each finding comes with a photo, its location, and a severity rating, so the case for spending is on paper.

In the report: photo evidence and ratings

Several sites, no common standard

Each branch, yard, or estate is secured differently, and nobody sees the whole picture.

We use the same checklist at every site, so findings can be compared and the worst sites fixed first.

Is this right for you?

A good fit, or better served by something else.

An audit is only worth paying for when it answers a question a quotation cannot.

This is a good fit if

  • You have had a break-in, theft, or loss and want to know how it happened.
  • You are about to spend significantly on guards, cameras, or access control and want a plan first.
  • You manage a large site or several sites and need one standard across them.
  • Management or a board needs written evidence before approving a budget.

You are better served elsewhere if

  • You only want prices for specific equipment. Book a free site survey
  • You need your yearly statutory fire safety audit. That must be done by an approved fire safety auditor. See Fire Alarm Systems
  • You need an IT or cyber security audit. This audit covers physical security only.

How much does a Corporate Risk Audit cost?

The fee depends on the size of the site, how many sites are included, and what the audit needs to cover. After a short scoping call, you receive the fee and scope in writing before anything is scheduled.

How does the fee credit work?

If you hire Urban Eye for any of the recommended work within 90 days of receiving your report, the full audit fee is credited against that work.

How long does the report take?

For a single site, the written report is delivered within 5 working days of the site inspection. For several sites, the delivery date is set in your written scope.

Do we have to use Urban Eye for the recommended work?

No. The report is yours. You can use it with Urban Eye, another installer, or your current guard company.

Is the report confidential?

Yes. The report, photos, and findings belong to your company and are shared only with the people you authorise.

How is this different from the free site survey?

The free site survey works out what equipment you need and what it will cost. The audit looks at where your site is exposed, including gaps equipment will not fix, such as guard routines and key control, and ranks what to fix first.
