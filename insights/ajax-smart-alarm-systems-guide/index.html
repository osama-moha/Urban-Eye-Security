How to compare alarm systems for your property

Compare alarm coverage, photo verification, connectivity and ongoing services before approving an installation.

Urban Eye Security Updated 22 October 2026 2 min read
Back to Insights

Compare the areas an alarm covers, the information it sends and who handles an alert. A device count alone does not answer those questions.

Start with the areas you need covered.

List the doors, windows, indoor rooms and outdoor approaches that matter. Ask the installer to mark detector positions and explain any areas outside the proposed coverage.

A detector at one entrance does not cover every window. Indoor motion detection and outdoor approaches require devices suited to their positions.

Decide whether you need alarm photographs.

A standard motion detector reports movement. A compatible photo detector can add images from its view. Check the hub, device compatibility and image access before choosing a plan.

Photos add context but do not eliminate false alarms. Placement, pets, weather and room use still affect performance. Indoor image-capturing devices are excluded from our short-stay rental configurations.

Check wiring, power and communications.

  • Which detectors need wiring, and where will cables run?
  • What backup is specified for the hub and connection equipment?
  • Has the installer tested radio and mobile coverage at the proposed positions?
  • Who checks batteries and connection faults after handover?

Separate connection from operator monitoring.

Urban Eye Connect is the required connection and support service for our alarm plans. You can handle app alerts yourself. Optional operators review supported events under an agreed monitoring plan.

Compare the recurring services and starting rates. Confirm setup charges, coverage and response arrangements before approval.

Ask for these quotation details.

  • Device models, quantities and planned positions.
  • Installation, configuration and user training.
  • Included equipment versus optional additions.
  • Required recurring charges and optional services.
  • Backup assumptions, maintenance and support responsibilities.
  • Administrator ownership, handover records and exclusions.
