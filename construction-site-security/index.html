Which of these sounds like you?

Site losses rarely look like a break-in. They look like a delivery.

These are the five problems project managers and developers bring to us most. Each one has a specific control, and the survey shows which your site needs.

Materials leaving without approval

Cement, steel, and tools go out through the gate, and nobody can say who approved it.

Gate records for deliveries, materials, and tools, with exit permits where the project requires them, checked by the guard at the gate.

Usually fits: gate and material control

Night theft from stores and the yard

The site goes quiet after work ends, and that is when stock disappears.

Wireless alarms on temporary stores and alerts along the perimeter, sent to your phone and, if you choose, to PRO Monitoring.

Usually fits: Equipment Purchase or Site Lease

No power or internet on site

Cameras need mains power and fibre that a new site does not have yet.

Solar-powered 4G cameras and a suitable battery-powered alarm design can serve sites without mains power. The survey checks signal, power requirements and servicing.

Usually fits: off-grid systems, quoted after the survey

A site that changes every few weeks

Security put in during groundworks is in the wrong place by fit-out.

Wireless devices are moved and repositioned as the project moves from structure to fit-out to handover.

Usually fits: Managed Site Lease

One guard and a big site

A single guard cannot watch every store, gate, and fence line at night.

Cameras and alarms support site guards. Optional operator monitoring reviews supported alerts and follows the agreed response procedures.

Usually fits: guarding with PRO Monitoring

How much does construction site security cost?

Choose equipment purchase or a managed lease with Ajax intrusion detection and two priority camera views. Your proposal confirms devices, positions, power, setup, data, storage, monitoring and maintenance. Guard posts and shifts are separately scoped.

Should I buy or lease site security?

Buy the equipment if you will manage the building afterwards or reuse the equipment on your next project. Lease it if you want Urban Eye to supply, install, and maintain the system for the length of the project as a monthly cost.

Can alarms and cameras move as the site changes?

Yes. Construction sites change by phase, so we review device positions, coverage, and access points as the project moves through structural build, fit-out, storage, and handover.

What if the site has no power or internet?

The survey checks solar exposure, cellular coverage and power needs. Off-grid options can combine solar-powered 4G cameras with a suitably powered Ajax Hub BP alarm design. Battery capacity, operating settings and servicing are specified for the site.

More questions (3)
Do Urban Eye guards manage material exit permits?

The site can require an approved exit permit before materials, tools, equipment, or scrap leave. The project manager agrees the procedure and approval contacts, and the guard records each exit against that process.

How is subcontractor access managed?

The site keeps a record of authorised subcontractors, visitors, and deliveries. The project manager sets the access rules, and Urban Eye applies them at the gate and in controlled areas.

Who responds to alarms at night?

With PRO Monitoring, operators review supported alerts and follow the agreed contact and response plan. Attendance and escalation depend on contracted coverage. The Managed Site Lease includes the monitoring hours and supported visual checks specified in the contract.
