Which of these sounds like you?

Warehouse losses hide in the gaps between the gate, the bay, and the stock room.

These are the five problems warehouse managers bring to us most. Each one points to a specific control, and the survey shows which your site needs first.

Stock that disappears between counts

The stock-take is short, and nobody can say which bay, shift, or vehicle it left on.

Cameras over loading bays and gates, with footage kept on site, show what left and when.

Usually fits: loading bay and gate cameras

Anyone can walk into the stock room

Staff, drivers, and contractors all pass the high-value cage during the day.

Access control on stock rooms and cages records who opened each door, with face or card readers where dust and worn hands defeat fingerprints.

Usually fits: Access Control

Break-ins through the yard at night

A long fence line and a dark yard give intruders hours of cover.

A free-standing electric fence split into zones, with fence alarms sent to your phone and to monitoring.

Usually fits: Electric Fencing

Fire alarms that dust keeps setting off

Dust triggers false alarms, so detectors end up covered or ignored.

EN 54 detectors with dust-protected chambers, sirens that sound together, and alerts to your phone when the building is empty.

Usually fits: Fire Alarm Systems

An alarm at 2 a.m. with nobody to act

An alarm at an empty warehouse only helps if someone responds to it.

Alerts reach our team and our response partner at the same moment, first responders attend, and serious incidents are escalated.

Is this right for your site?

A good fit, or better served by something else.

We would rather point you to the right service than scope a warehouse plan for a site that does not need one.

A warehouse plan is a good fit if

  • You run a warehouse, distribution centre, cold store, or industrial yard.
  • Stock losses show up at stock-take with no explanation.
  • Many people and vehicles pass through your gates and bays.
  • The site is empty or lightly staffed at night.

You are better served elsewhere if

  • You only need to protect a shop and its stockroom. See Shop Security
  • You are protecting an active building project. See Construction Site Security

How do you secure loading bays and roller shutters?

It depends on the door type, operating hours, nearby routes, and stock exposure. During the survey we decide whether each bay needs camera coverage, door monitoring, motion detection, lighting, access rules, or a combination.

Can wireless alarms work in a large warehouse?

Wireless range depends on the building, steel, concrete, pallet racking, stock levels, and where devices are placed. We confirm coverage during the survey and test it at installation, and add range extenders where the site needs them.

Will dust set off the fire alarm?

The EN 54-certified smoke detectors we install have dust-protected smoke chambers and a dual-spectrum sensor that tells real smoke apart from steam and vapour. Where dust is heavy, the survey also decides where a heat detector is the better choice.

Can you reduce warehouse shrinkage?

We cannot promise a figure, but we can close the gaps that let it happen. The survey finds weak movement points and gaps in access, detection, and records, and the system gives your team the footage and door logs to investigate discrepancies.

Can guards and technology work together?

Yes. Guards control the gate and vehicles, while cameras, alarms, and fencing cover the bays, stock rooms, and perimeter that one guard cannot watch at the same time. Guarding is quoted separately after the survey.
