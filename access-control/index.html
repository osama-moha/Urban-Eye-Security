Which of these sounds like you?

Access control is worth it when it fixes a problem you already have.

These are the five situations we are asked to solve most. If yours is here, a survey will show exactly which doors need control.

Keys that are copied, lost, or never returned

A former employee still has a key, and nobody knows how many copies exist.

Cancel a card or fingerprint in the software and it stops working at every door. Each door records who opened it and when, so a missing item has a trail.

Usually fits: offices, clinics, and stores

Staff clocking in for each other

Payroll pays for hours nobody worked, because a PIN or card can be handed to a friend.

Biometric credentials can strengthen assurance that the enrolled individual is presenting the credential, but performance depends on reader choice, enrolment quality, lighting, user conditions, and system configuration. Because biometric data is sensitive personal data, biometric access-control projects should be designed with a documented lawful basis, appropriate safeguards, a privacy assessment where required, clear staff communication, retention rules, and a practical non-biometric option such as an encrypted card or mobile credential where appropriate.

Usually fits: offices, factories, and warehouses

Visitors walking into areas they should not

The visitor badge opens every door, or none, so staff keep walking down to reception.

Suprema's visitor module issues a card or QR pass that opens only the doors and lift floors you assign, for the period of the visit, with no paper register on the counter.

Usually fits: shared office buildings and headquarters

Fingerprint readers that reject your workers

Dust, grease, and worn fingers cause failed scans, queues at shift change, and doors propped open.

Where hands are dirty or worn, we specify face recognition or encrypted cards instead of fingerprints, so the door works for the people who use it every day.

Usually fits: factories, workshops, and warehouses

Exit doors that trap people in a fire

A poorly specified access-controlled escape door can obstruct safe egress.

Escape-route doors must be assessed and designed for immediate opening from inside, with lock type, emergency release, backup power, and any fire-alarm interface assessed during the survey.

Usually fits: pair with a fire alarm system

At every controlled door

What happens between a scan and an open door.

The same sequence runs at every door, whether it is the main entrance, a store room, or a lift.

01

A person presents a credential

A fingerprint, face, encrypted card, QR code, phone, or PIN, depending on what the door is set up to accept.

02

The system checks permission

It confirms who the person is, whether they may use this door, and whether it is within their allowed hours.

03

The door opens, or stays locked

Authorised people go through. Refused attempts are recorded, so repeated tries at a restricted room stand out.

04

Every event is logged

You can see who went through which door and when, and cancel a lost card or a departed employee straight away.

05

In an emergency, doors release

Where appropriate, access-control systems can be interfaced with a correctly specified fire-alarm and emergency-egress design. Final operation is confirmed door by door during the survey and commissioning process.

The hardware

Suprema or Ajax, chosen for your site.

We install two systems because no single one fits every building. The survey decides which, and we explain why.

Suprema: Biometric Entry, Visitors, and Larger Sites

BioStation 3 readers accept face, card, and QR code credentials, and CoreStation controllers manage many doors from one system. The BioStar 2 visitor module issues visitor passes, OM‑120 modules limit which lift floors a person can reach, and a fire alarm zone unlocks its doors when your fire alarm signals.

Ajax: Keypad, Card, and Phone Entry With Your Alarm

Ajax KeyPad TouchScreen accepts codes, DESFire cards and tags, and phones over Bluetooth. Relay modules switch electric locks, gates, and barriers, and everything runs in the same Ajax app as your alarm and fire system.

Cards That Cannot Simply Be Copied

Basic proximity cards can be cloned with cheap tools. Ajax DESFire credentials combine 128‑bit encryption with copy protection, and Suprema readers can connect over OSDP Secure Channel, which encrypts the reader-to-controller link with AES‑128.

Is access control right for you?

A good fit, or better served by something else.

We would rather point you to the right solution than sell you doors you do not need.

Access control is a good fit if

  • Keys change hands between staff, cleaners, and contractors.
  • You have rooms only some people should enter: server rooms, stores, pharmacies, or cash offices.
  • Visitors or tenants share your entrance and should not reach every floor.
  • You need a record of who went where, and when.

You are better served elsewhere if

  • You are protecting a single family home. See Residential Security
  • You need someone at the gate checking vehicles and deliveries. See Manned Guarding
  • You mainly need to watch an open yard or compound. See CCTV Installation
  • You only want a standalone keypad with no records. A full system is more than you need.

Do we need a paid Health Check before installing access control?

No. A free survey is appropriate when you are installing a new system or adding clearly defined doors. The paid Health Check is for an existing or inherited system where Urban Eye must diagnose faults, assess emergency egress, review administration and access governance, or establish the scope of a system takeover before recommending work.

How much does an access control system cost?

Every system is quoted after a site survey. The price depends on how many doors need control, the reader type (card, fingerprint, or face), the lock on each door, whether you need visitor passes or lift floor control, and the cabling and backup power each door needs. You receive a written quotation listing every door before anything is installed.

Is it legal to use fingerprints or face recognition for staff access?

It can be, if it is set up properly. Kenya's Data Protection Act treats biometric data as sensitive personal data, and processing that is likely to be high risk needs a data protection impact assessment before it starts. The Data Protection Commissioner's draft guidance for private security says staff consent is needed unless there is a real safety or security risk, and that people should be offered another way in, such as a card. This is a summary, not legal advice.

Can we use fingerprint attendance for payroll?

Only with its own lawful basis. The Data Protection Commissioner's draft guidance says biometric access justified for security should not be reused to monitor attendance or performance. If you want biometric attendance, plan for staff consent, a data protection impact assessment, and a card option for anyone who declines. Many businesses use card-based attendance instead, which avoids biometric data altogether.

What happens to the doors during a power cut or a fire?

Doors on escape routes use fail-safe locks that release when power is cut, and Suprema systems can take a signal from your fire alarm to unlock every door in a fire alarm zone. Under the Occupational Safety and Health Act, exit doors must be easy to open immediately from the inside while people are at work. Store rooms and server rooms that are not on an escape route can use fail-secure locks that stay locked. Backup power for each door is sized during the survey.

How do visitors get in without a paper register?

Reception registers the visitor in Suprema's BioStar 2 visitor module and issues a card or a QR code pass. The pass opens only the doors and lift floors you assign, for the period of the visit. You decide how long visitor records are kept, which matters because the Data Protection Commissioner's draft guidance says records should not be kept just in case.

Will we get Suprema or Ajax?

It depends on the site. Ajax suits a few doors opened with a code, card, or phone, especially where you already have an Ajax alarm or fire system. Suprema suits fingerprint or face entry, visitor passes, lift control, and buildings with many doors. We recommend one after the survey and explain why.

Can access cards be copied?

Basic proximity cards can be copied with cheap tools. Ajax keypads use DESFire cards and tags, which combine 128-bit encryption with copy protection. On Suprema systems, readers can connect over OSDP Secure Channel, which encrypts the link between reader and controller with AES-128.
